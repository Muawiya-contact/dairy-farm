# Step 6 — Wusooli aur Galla

## What the owner can do

- View each account's monthly opening balance, money in, money out, closing balance, and dated ledger on `/galla/`. A closed account remains visible while it has a carried balance. A negative balance is shown as a warning, never silently changed.
- An account opened after the first day of a month shows its starting amount on the actual opening date in the ledger. Future opening dates are rejected.
- Move money between two active accounts with one transfer entry. The source and destination ledger update together. A bank-type account is only a **nickname** such as “Farm bank”; the app stores no bank account number, credentials, or bank API connection. The owner enters every transaction manually.
- Count physical cash on any unlocked date through today. The database records the counted amount, calculated ledger amount, difference, and reason. A nonzero difference requires a reason. Counts are immutable snapshots; a later correction is a new count. Cash counts do not change the ledger balance.
- Record a customer discount (riayat) without adding account cash. Correct or void a manually entered payment or discount with a required reason. The audit table retains before and after values, customer, time, and actor. Naqd receipts generated by a milk delivery must be corrected through the milk shift.
- Export payment audits and cash counts with the existing CSV, Excel, PDF, and JSON data exports in Settings.

## Data and access design

`account_entries(uuid)` and `account_month_summary(date)` remain the ledger source of truth. The new `account_balance_on(uuid,date)` calculates expected cash on a date. `cash_counts` holds reconciliation snapshots; `payment_audit` holds immutable payment-change history; `payments.revision` prevents an outdated correction from overwriting a newer one. `correct_manual_payment` and `void_manual_payment` are database transactions that validate the expected revision, set the audit reason, and make the change atomically. Month-lock triggers reject changes to closed periods, including moving an entry into or out of one.

The browser fetches a selected account's entries and cash counts by month in pages of 1,000; it requests one customer's monthly report instead of loading all reports to find it. Money is stored in PostgreSQL `numeric(12,2)` fields. Browser validation rejects negative and overprecise amounts; database constraints reject negative amounts, and the correction RPC also rejects overprecision. Transfers, payments, discounts, and cash counts carry client-generated UUIDs so a retry after an uncertain network result can recognize the already saved row.

Staging grants these operations to its shared anonymous demo role and must contain fictional data only. Production keeps row level security for the designated owner; there is no database switch in the browser. Migrations `20261014000021` through `20261014000023` were applied with Supabase CLI migration history to **both** projects.

## Validation and operating notes

The PGlite database tests cover transfer balance conservation, future and inactive-account rejection, count differences and immutability, discount accounting, correction/void audit, stale revisions, Naqd protection, locked months, owner access, and closed accounts with a balance. Unit tests cover input validation and month boundaries. Run `npm run check` for the full suite, lint, TypeScript, and static build. Verify the Netlify Deploy Preview on phone and laptop before merging.

The owner should count cash at the end of a farm day, compare it to the calculated balance, and investigate a discrepancy before recording a new count. A later entry for that date can make an earlier snapshot stale; record another count rather than changing the old one. Closing an account does not clear its balance. Bank balances are never fetched automatically.
